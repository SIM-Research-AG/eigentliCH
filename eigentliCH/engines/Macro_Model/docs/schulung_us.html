The one thing to take away

United States is in Saturation and reordering, and it is there because K_R/K_I = 0.940 is below 1.00. Its saturation of 3.48 is inside the balanced band, so the phase does not come from the saturation level. Anyone who reports the phase and the band position without saying which of them decided the classification has told half the story.

Where the economy sits

The saturation axis, its band, and the crossing history.

Saturation ran from 1.89 in 1972 to a peak of 4.11 in 2020, and stands at 3.48 in 2024, that is 0.62 below the peak. It first entered the band in 1988 and first crossed the upper bound of 3.5 in 2009, spending 14 of 53 years at or above it.

Read this before quoting the level

The saturation axis carries a credit uplift of 1.40, because the published BIS series omits credit to the financial sector and captures non-bank intermediation incompletely. The level on this chart is therefore not a published figure. The uplift is multiplicative and applied identically to every economy, so it cannot change a growth rate, a direction, a turning point, or the ordering of the cross-section. What it changes is where the economy sits against the band, which is exactly its purpose.

Why the phase is what it is

The condition that actually fired.

Real capital stood at 0.94 times financial capital in 2024, against 2.06 in 1972. It fell below one in 2015 and has stayed there for 10 of the years since. That condition, and not the saturation level, is what places the economy in its current phase.

This ratio matters out of proportion to its simplicity: it is a ratio of two capital stocks, so it is invariant to every level adjustment the programme applies. The credit uplift and the capital normalisation both scale numerator and denominator alike. It is the one phase condition the scale problem cannot reach, which is why it is trustworthy where a level is not.

The financial economy against the real one

Two measures, a common turning point, and one identity worth knowing.

Capital saturation and the unsecured-asset gap measure different things. They agree on when the financial economy turned: saturation peaks in 2020 and the gap in 2020, and both have retraced since without returning to their pre-2000 levels. The gap ran from 0.27 of output in 1972 to a peak of 0.66 in 2020 and stands at 0.48.

Price divergence is the unsecured-asset gap, not a second opinion on it

The derived indicator "asset prices against real prices" stands at 1.80, against 1.00 in 1972 and a peak of 2.48 in 2020. Indexed to its first year it is the same series as the unsecured-asset gap, with a measured correlation of 1.000000 on this run.

That is an identity rather than a coincidence, and it follows from the derivation in book chapter 7. Splitting the quantity equation into a real and a financial sector and holding both transaction frequencies constant gives P_R . H_R = Y and P_I . H_I = K_R + K_I - Y, so the ratio of the two price levels is (K_R + K_I - Y) / Y up to a constant, which is the definition of the gap.

The practical consequence: do not quote both as independent evidence. They are one measurement expressed in two units, and treating them as two agreeing indicators would double-count it. Price divergence is therefore omitted from the chart above rather than drawn on top of the gap.

Where the economy sits in the cycles

Two cycles are estimated. Two are anchored, because they cannot be estimated.

The capital cycle cannot be estimated from 53 years of data, since a 90-year cycle needs roughly two of its own periods. It is anchored instead: reaching a saturation of 3.5 places an economy 90 years into the cycle. United States last crossed 3.5 in 2013.5, which puts it 100 years into a 90-year cycle, that is about 10 years past the reordering point.

The innovation cycle is anchored the same way and for the same reason, on a low in 2032 with a period of 47 years, applied identically to every economy because it is a global technological cycle rather than a national one. On this vintage that low is 8 years ahead.

This is the reordering, not a failure of the model

A projection that decays a saturated economy to a low capital base, ending in the least saturated phase, is the capital-cycle reordering. Book section 20.3 states it: the capital cycle "is directional. It progresses through the phase structure, terminates in saturation, and is followed by a reordering that resets the system at a lower capital base rather than returning it to the state it left." Chapter 8 gives the mechanism, that the claims of financial capital eventually exceed what real output can provide and the system must reset them. Reaching the foundation phase after a reset is the framework's prediction, not a contradiction of it.

The two resolutions, debt deflation and hyperinflation, are two routes to that same reset. The model does not choose between them.

Every adjustment here is a multiplicative level scale, and that is a guarantee rather than a convention: scaling a series by a constant leaves its growth rates, its direction and its turning points identical, because the derivative of a log is unchanged by a constant factor. No adjustment this programme can produce is capable of altering any of the primary criteria above. An additive drift correction would be capable of it, and is therefore not implemented at all rather than guarded.

The capital normalisation, and the condition it hides

Both capital stocks are scaled by 0.2187, a common factor, so that the highest capital-to-output ratio over the window falls from 4.11 to 0.90. Without it the investment share r = 1 - K_R/Y turns negative, the reduced output equation drives output towards zero, and the system does not integrate at all.

Because the factor is applied to both stocks, K_R/K_I is unchanged, which is why the phase condition above survives it. Being multiplicative, it also cannot change a growth rate, a direction or a turning point.

What it does cost: K_R/Y can no longer cross one inside the window, so the production-versus-financial transition is not observable on the normalised path. If someone asks where this economy sits on that boundary, the honest answer is that this run cannot say.

What this reading does not include

Stated, so that its absence is not mistaken for a finding.

  • The stock-to-gold model is switched off. Its module and tests are retained and correct, and it is simply not part of the current output. Nothing in this document depends on it.
  • The Health of Nations Indicator is not scored here. Its fifteen indicators are not derivable from the three-body state and the data layer does not yet read the published export, so no composite is produced. A composite computed from a thin subset would be worse than none.
  • The 25-state regime distribution is not produced. The kernel apparatus is complete, but nothing yet assesses the four segments from indicators, and inventing the segment assessments would produce a picture of the kernels rather than of the economy.
  • Only the two short cycles are estimated. The other two are anchored from supplied structure, and the tables above mark which is which.

Data caveats carried by this run

  • the real-capital ratio is published through 2019 and extended to 2024 in ratio space. The extended values are computed, not published, and any output using them carries the extension label.
  • the credit saturation axis carries an uplift of 1.40, because BIS total credit to the non-financial sector omits credit to the financial sector and much non-bank intermediation. The uplift flows into K_I, since K_I is the saturation ratio times output. It is a level adjustment, so growth rates, direction and turning points are unchanged, and it is applied identically to every economy, so the cross-section ordering is unchanged.
  • K_I is BIS total credit to the non-financial sector only. Listed equity market capitalisation is excluded because it is not comparable across economies and its vintages differ by up to seven years, so including it would make a cross-section incoherent.
  • the highest published capital-to-output ratio over the window is 4.11, which makes the investment share r = 1 - K_R/Y equal -3.11 at that point. Wherever r is negative the reduced output equation Y_dot = (p_b Y + r K_I + S) / 2 drives output towards zero, so the system does not integrate. A common scale of 0.2187 applied to both capital stocks brings the maximum K_R/Y to 0.90, so r stays positive for every period and section 0.2's definition of it as a share holds throughout. The scale is applied to both stocks, so K_R/K_I and therefore the Phase 4 condition are unchanged, and being multiplicative it cannot alter growth rates, direction or turning points. The consequence is that K_R/Y cannot cross one inside the calibration window, so the production-versus-financial transition of section 0.5 is not observable on the normalised path.
  • K_R is reconstructed as the Penn World Table capital-to-output ratio multiplied by market-valued output, so it is market-valued by construction and no PPP conversion factor is involved. It is a reconstruction rather than a published level.
