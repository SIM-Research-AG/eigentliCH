1The idea

Institutional asset–liability management measures success not by cash on hand, but by whether the assets will meet the liabilities as they fall due — under uncertainty, with a margin of safety. We apply exactly that question to a single life. Three re-framings carry the model:

  • Capitals are assets. Not only money — also expertise, network and health. Each carries value across time, yields a return, and depreciates without maintenance.
  • Goals are liabilities. A home, founding a company, financial independence, retirement — each a claim: an amount, due at a date, with a required probability of success.
  • Time is the binding constraint — the saving rate is an output. Money can be borrowed and compounded; the 24-hour day cannot. And you don't set the saving rate as a dial — it falls out of income minus consumption and self-investment.
Definition — liability

A goal is a triple (deadline, region, confidence): a time by which it must be met; a set of states that count as meeting it; and the probability of success required. A goal with no required confidence is a wish, not a liability.

2The state: four capitals

The state is everything we must carry forward in time. Wealth is deliberately split three ways, because a single number cannot tell an honest financial story.

SymbolCapitalBehaviour
W_LLiquid wealth (cash, securities)accumulates, earns a return
W_RReal / illiquid assets (property)appreciates, yields, illiquid
DDebt (mortgage)amortises; jumps on borrowing
EExpertise / human capitalS-curve (logistic)
NNetwork / social capitalS-curve; ceiling rises with E and W
HHealth / energyS-curve; a multiplier on everything
κStandard-of-living habit (a stock, not a capital)relaxes toward recent consumption; lifts the network ceiling

The characteristic motion of each capital is the S-curve: slow while small; accelerating once large enough to self-reinforce; flattening at a natural ceiling. Wealth, by contrast, accumulates without a ceiling. This is why timing matters so much — the same effort produces almost nothing in the slow phase and a great deal in the accelerating one.

The four capitals are joined by a standard-of-living habit κ, carried as an extra stock and on by default. It tracks recent consumption via dκ = α(C − κ) dt, and it is not a bare reference point: a maintained lifestyle keeps a person in the rooms and memberships that lift the network ceiling, so a cut in spending is felt as a loss and also lowers a ceiling. An optional macro-regime layer can be switched on (off by default) to couple the plan to the capital cycle; see the engine section.

3The controls: two budgets

You cannot move the capitals directly. You move them by allocating time and money each period.

BudgetLevers
Time (τ)Shares for earning (τ_Y), learning (τ_E), networking (τ_N), recovery (τ_H). Sum ≤ 1; the rest is leisure.
MoneyConsumption C, education spend m_E, network spend m_N, amortisation p_A.
w / θPortfolio over four roles — Gain, Income, Stabilization, Protection (cash the residual). With the regime layer off this collapses to a single risk tilt θ (risky vs safe); with it on, the four roles rotate as the cycle turns.

Note: consumption is the lever — the saving rate falls out of it. Set the saving rate directly and you have merely hidden the real decision. The portfolio roles are the same taxonomy the institutional Optimizer uses, so a personal plan and a fund share one calibration.

4Coupling & feedback loops

The capitals are wired together. The single most consequential wire: expertise, wealth and a maintained standard of living raise the ceiling of the network — competence, resources and the habit open doors that would otherwise stay shut (N̄ = N₀ + λ_E E + λ_W W + λ_κ κ). The habit coefficient λ_κ is the least identifiable parameter and is held small.

Two loops deserve names:

  • Reinforcing loop: expertise → income → wealth → network ceiling → income. Success compounds.
  • Balancing loop: earning → overwork → lower health → lower income. The system resists being pushed too hard.
Takeaway

A healthy plan rides the reinforcing loop without tripping the balancing one. That line is exactly what the optimiser looks for.

5Goals as liabilities

Four archetypes cover most life goals. Each is a region of the state space, with a deadline and a required confidence.

ArchetypeCondition
HomeLump claim and serviceability: enough deposit and income to carry the mortgage at a stressed rate.
CompanyJoint region: cash buffer and network and expertise above threshold simultaneously — no substitution between them.
RetirementLiability stream: wealth plus the present value of future income covers the present value of future spending (funding ratio ≥ 1).
IndependenceCounterfactual: set labour income to zero — do passive returns plus a safe drawdown cover desired spending? Only drawable wealth counts: W_L + h_R W_R − D, with the self-occupied residence at h_home = 0 and human capital excluded (independence is the state of not needing it).
Definition — feasibility as a probability

The future is not a line. Feasibility is therefore not yes/no but a probability: we simulate many futures (Monte-Carlo) and count the fraction that reach the goal by the deadline. That number is the gauge you see in the app. Inside the optimiser it is replaced by a convex, conservative surrogate — the CVaR (Conditional Value-at-Risk) of the shortfall.

6The engine

A consumer cannot be handed a 40-year plan. The engine works as Model-Predictive Control (MPC) over a receding horizon:

  • From today's state, sample many scenarios and solve a finite-horizon optimisation.
  • Apply only this period's action — the rest stays a plan.
  • Next period: observe what actually happened and re-solve. “Life happened” simply becomes the new starting point.

From the problem's shadow prices (the cost of the state transitions) comes the most differentiated output: the exchange rate between levers.

Example — exchange rate

“An hour of networking moves you toward your goal about 1.8× faster than an hour of overtime.” This ranking is not a rule of thumb — it is computed from the state and shifts as the state shifts. With a saturated network, overtime often wins; when founding a company, networking wins.

Taxes are built in (Swiss-style: progressive income tax with interest deduction, plus a wealth tax) and reduce net inflows. They are shown explicitly in the app.

Reliability — the two-phase solve

Hard goals used to make a single-pass optimiser give up and collapse the plan (probability jumping between ~100% and ~0%). The engine now solves in two phases: first it restores feasibility (how well can the goal be funded?), then it maximises the life subject to that achievable funding bound. The result responds smoothly to the inputs instead of flipping between “achieve” and “give up”.

Optional — the regime layer

Switched on, the four role returns become phase-dependent, so the portfolio rotates across Gain / Income / Stabilization / Protection as the macro cycle turns — the same cycle the institutional Optimizer uses. Switched off (the default), returns are constant and the portfolio is the scalar tilt θ.

7Using the app

Launch via the desktop shortcut Life Balance Sheet. A console window starts the engine, the browser shows the interface. Close the window to stop the app.

Inputs (left)

  • Person & wealth: age, liquid / real wealth, debt.
  • Capitals: expertise, network, health on a 0–1 scale (1 = fully developed).
  • Goals — one or more: type, years to deadline, confidence and type-specific parameters. Add several at once with “+ Add goal”.
  • Load an example (the five worked lives: young professional, independence by fifty, near-retiree, entrepreneur, tail shock) and save your own users.

Results (right)

  • Goal gauges: one probability per goal against its confidence target, plus the binding constraint.
  • Trajectory: the S-curves of expertise, network and health over time.
  • This period's action in plain words, and the plan over time (work, save/invest per year, consumption, risk tilt).
  • Taxes, investment strategy (mock), the one insight (exchange rate), and the capitals' shadow prices.

A calculation typically takes 10–30 seconds — the engine solves a genuine stochastic optimal-control problem and runs Monte-Carlo.

8Honest limits & current status

  • Numbers are illustrative. The value is comparative (this plan vs. that one) and in the binding constraint — which lever matters — not in the exact percentage.
  • The worked lives are calibrated to their mechanism. Each is set so the right binding constraint bites at a sensible target confidence; the exact percentage is illustrative. Custom inputs run the same engine.
  • Solver reliability is addressed by the two-phase solve. Results are now stable across scenario counts rather than flipping between “achieve” and “give up”. Very hard or genuinely infeasible goals still report a low probability — honestly, not as a bug.
  • Not financial, tax or legal advice. A tool for thinking — to make trade-offs visible, with time and health on the balance sheet and goals as claims that can be missed.
NoteSome inputs are simply infeasible (e.g. too large a goal in too little time). The app then deliberately shows 0% / “below target” — that is the model being honest, not a bug.
